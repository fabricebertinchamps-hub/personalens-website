Is it safe to upload your WhatsApp chat to an AI website?

It can be, but "safe" depends entirely on the site. The moment you export a WhatsApp chat, it stops being end-to-end encrypted and becomes an ordinary file, and whatever site you upload it to decides how long it is kept, who else processes it, and whether it trains anyone's model. A trustworthy site answers those questions plainly in its privacy policy. If you cannot find the answers in two minutes, do not upload.

What actually happens when you upload a chat

It helps to know the path the text takes, because every step is a place where something could be kept.

  1. The export. WhatsApp messages are end-to-end encrypted inside the app. Export chat produces a plain-text file. From here on, WhatsApp's encryption no longer applies. (Our export guide covers this step.)
  2. The upload. A reputable site sends the file over HTTPS, so it cannot be read in transit. That part is now standard and tells you little.
  3. Processing. To produce an AI reading, the text has to be read by a language model running on a server. That server may belong to the site, or more often to a model provider the site pays. This is the step most privacy pages are vague about.
  4. Storage. The site may keep the original chat, the reading, both, or neither. It may keep logs. It may keep them for a day or indefinitely.
  5. Secondary use. The text may or may not be used to train or improve models, reviewed by staff for quality, or shared with analytics and advertising partners.

Safe, in practice, means: minimal storage, no secondary use, named processors, and a way to delete what is kept.

The 10-point checklist

Run this on any site before uploading, including ours.

1. Is there a real privacy policy, written for this product?

Not a generic template that talks about "services" and "customers" without ever mentioning chats or messages. A site that analyses conversations should say what happens to conversations.

2. Does it say how long your chat is kept?

Look for a specific answer: not retained after processing, deleted after 24 hours, kept until you delete your account. "As long as necessary" is not an answer.

3. Does it say whether your chat trains AI models?

This should be an explicit no, or an explicit opt-in. Silence here usually means yes, or that nobody has checked.

4. Does it name who processes the text?

If a third-party AI model reads your chat, the policy should say so, and ideally name the provider or category of provider and the terms that apply (for example, that the provider does not train on API data). Under the EU's GDPR, companies must disclose recipients or categories of recipients of personal data.

5. Does it need an account, email or phone number?

Every identifier you hand over is something that can link the chat to you. The safest designs need none.

6. Where does the result live?

A reading stored in your own browser or device is very different from a reading stored in a cloud database tied to your email.

7. Can you delete it?

There should be a clear way to delete anything kept, and a contact address that actually answers.

8. Who runs the site?

A named company or person, a country, a contact address. Anonymous sites with no legal page are a hard pass for something this personal.

9. What does it ask for beyond the file?

A file upload or pasted text is normal. Requests for your WhatsApp login, a QR code to scan, contacts access, or a browser extension that reads WhatsApp Web are not. See the red flags below.

10. Does the business model add up?

If the analysis is free and there is no paid tier, ask how the site pays for model costs. Sometimes the honest answer is that it is a hobby project. Sometimes it is data.

Red flags that should stop you

  • "Scan this QR code to connect WhatsApp." A WhatsApp Web QR code links a new device to your account. A third party holding a linked device can potentially read your chats on an ongoing basis, not just the one you meant to share. Never scan a WhatsApp QR code for a website you do not fully trust. (You can review and remove linked devices in WhatsApp under Settings, Linked devices.)
  • Browser extensions that read WhatsApp Web. These can see every chat you open, every time, for as long as the extension is installed.
  • Marketing about reading someone else's messages. "See who your partner is talking to." "Monitor their WhatsApp." This is surveillance, often illegal, and in abusive relationships it is a recognised tool of control.
  • Mandatory sign-up before you can see the privacy policy.
  • Promises that sound like certainty. "100% accurate lie detection" tells you how seriously the site treats everything else.

How to upload more safely, whatever site you use

  • Upload less. If your question is about the last three months, you do not need the last five years. Shorter is safer, though too short gives a weak reading; see how many messages you need.
  • Remove what does not belong. Addresses, bank details, passwords, ID numbers, medical details and other people's secrets have no analytical value. Delete those lines before uploading.
  • Mind special categories. Chats about health, sexuality, religion or legal trouble are especially sensitive, and in the EU they count as special-category data. Think twice before uploading them anywhere.
  • Delete the export afterwards from your downloads, email and cloud drive.
  • Use a private browser window if you share the device.

The person who is not in the room

A chat has at least two authors. Your half is yours to share; theirs was written for you, not for a server. Privacy scholars call this contextual integrity (Nissenbaum, 2004): information carries the expectations of the context it was shared in. Uploading a conversation for your own understanding, briefly, to a service that does not keep it, is a modest step outside that context. Uploading it to a site that stores it, trains on it, or lets you share the result widely is a much bigger one. Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to upload a WhatsApp chat to an AI analyser?

It depends on the site. Look for a clear privacy policy that states how long the chat is kept, that it is not used to train models, who processes the text, and whether an account is needed. If those answers are missing, do not upload.

Is an exported WhatsApp chat still end-to-end encrypted?

No. Encryption protects messages inside WhatsApp. Once exported, the chat is a plain-text file, and its protection depends on where you store it and which services you upload it to.

Should I ever scan a QR code to connect WhatsApp to an analysis website?

Avoid it unless you fully trust the service. A WhatsApp QR code links a new device to your account, which can give ongoing access to your chats. Uploading an exported file shares only that one conversation.

Do AI chat analysers use my conversations to train their models?

Some do and some do not. A trustworthy site states this explicitly in its privacy policy. If the policy is silent on training, assume the answer may be yes.

How can I make uploading a chat safer?

Upload only the period you need, delete addresses, financial details, health information and other people's secrets first, use a service that needs no account, and delete the exported file from your device and email afterwards.
